# Decision log

Changes relative to *Dynamic Risk Management of Agency MBS: Project Design* (the design PDF, Nov 1, 2026). Each entry says what was retained, simplified, deferred, or replaced, and why. Dates are when the decision was made. Entries 1 to 23 precede any real-data run. Entries 24 and 25 were made after the first development run and before any test-period result was computed.

## Material changes

| # | Date | Design PDF | This project | Status | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2026-11-02 | Research question about the share of duration variation that can be forecast | Question reframed around the decision: when does refinancing information improve on an updating hedge, after costs | Refined | The decision framing makes the updating hedge the benchmark that matters and keeps every outcome interpretable for a portfolio manager. |
| 2 | 2026-11-02 | State model linear in the incentive (F3) | State model linear in the share of borrowers in the money, g(I) = Φ((I − θ)/σ) | Replaced | Duration saturates deep in and out of the money. The bounded form keeps two fitted parameters and reads as a weighted average of a locked-in duration and an in-the-money duration. |
| 3 | 2026-11-02 | Hybrid ARX: a + ρ·(last realized duration) + c·I | Hybrid = updating estimate + ĉ₁·(g(I) − ḡ), where ḡ uses the updating regression's own weights | Replaced | The ARX hybrid mixes two effects (shrinkage toward the mean and new information). The new hybrid nests the updating hedge exactly and adds no free parameter, so H vs U isolates the refinancing information. |
| 4 | 2026-11-02 | Pre-2013 proxy fitted on 2013–2014 NMDB used as a historical signal | Proxy used for development and teaching only, every such forecast labeled `proxy_retrospective`, and a robustness run trains the state model only on NMDB months | Replaced | A proxy calibrated with 2013–2014 data was not available to a 2010–2012 decision-maker. |

The live NMDB file (verified 2026-11-02) carries these market segments, and UPB weighting matches how an MBS index weights borrowers. |
| 6 | 2026-11-02 | Base execution at the decision close | Base execution at the next trading day's close, with 0-day and 5-day sensitivity | Changed | The exposure estimate uses that day's close, so trading at the same close is optimistic. |

| 8 | 2026-11-02 | "Survives costs" via after-cost information ratio and break-even cost | Declared risk-cost objective: value volatility reduction at a hurdle information ratio of 0.5, plus a statistical-support requirement, plus break-even cost | Changed | Mean hedged returns are dominated by exposure luck (realized rate moves times hedge differences), which makes information-ratio comparisons noisy and misleading. |
| 9 | 2026-11-02 | Diebold–Mariano on squared weekly hedged returns, Clark–West, Holm over four comparisons, power analysis | Paired block bootstrap intervals carry the weight. Power and false-positive rate come from full-pipeline simulation. | Simplified | Fewer tests, each matched to an actual nesting and loss. |
| 10 | 2026-11-02 | Twelve core notebooks plus stretch notebooks | Eleven notebooks (00–10): exposure, forecasts, backtest, results, episodes, external validation, and handoff, with results and stress episodes merged | Simplified | Smallest coherent teaching sequence. |
| 11 | 2026-11-02 | PIMCO predecessor fund (PTRIX) as a held-out asset with daily NAV from Yahoo | Optional extension that runs only on user-supplied NAV history, plus a descriptive PMBS comparison since September 2024 | Deferred | Verified 2026-11-02: Yahoo now maps PTRIX and PMRPX to the PMBS ETF and returns no history. | 12 | 2026-11-02 | Git tag `prereg-v1` as a public timestamped preregistration | Local freeze record with SHA-256 hashes of the experiment configuration, result-relevant code, and development selections, plus a test-evaluation log. | 13 | 2026-11-02 | Refinanceable share from NMDB buckets as a robustness variable | Rate buckets used to estimate the dispersion σ of note rates (development quarters only) and to validate the normal approximation | Refined | The open top bucket (≥ 6%) cannot resolve the refinanceable share when PMMS is above 6%. |
| 14 | 2026-11-02 | Monthly WLS weights 1/(s² + τ²) for state models | Retained | Retained | Downweights noisy months such as late 2008. |
| 15 | 2026-11-02 | Newey–West (2 lags) standard errors for monthly durations | Newey–West (2 lags) with the HC3 leverage adjustment | Changed | Synthetic known-truth runs (no real data) showed plain Newey–West intervals covering the true monthly duration only about 85% of the time with about 21 days a month, because days with large yield moves have high leverage and shrunken residuals. |
| 16 | 2026-11-02 | Cochran's Q against χ², DerSimonian–Laird τ, and a constant-duration bootstrap | Bootstrap p-value as the test, χ² reported only as a naive reference, and a robust τ from the variance in excess of the null | Refined | In constant-duration synthetic worlds the χ² version rejected in most worlds (even with leverage-adjusted errors it rejected about a quarter of the time), while the bootstrap rejected near the nominal rate. |
| 17 | 2026-11-02 | Outcome map with rows for null and positive results | Four pre-declared outcomes, adding "small but statistically supported improvement" | Refined | A gain that survives costs with statistical support but falls below the 5% practical target should not be labeled "no gain". Mapping fixed in `docs/outcome_interpretations.md` before any real-data run. |
| 18 | 2026-11-02 | Power analysis and a minimum detectable effect from null-world spreads, with "inconclusive" meaning an effect below that MDE | Three kinds of synthetic world: duration driven by refinancing, constant duration, and duration that moves as much but independently of refinancing (the reference null for the hybrid against the updating hedge). Notebook 04 reports the false-positive rate in the reference null worlds and the power at the effect size of the refinancing worlds, measured directly. On real data, a no-gain result is inconclusive when its 95% interval still reaches the 5% target. | Changed | An independent code review (synthetic runs only) found that a detectable effect computed from the null spread promised far more power than the test has: when the hybrid really differs from the updating hedge, the test is about ten times noisier than when the two nearly coincide. The test period's own interval answers the practical question directly. |
| 19 | 2026-11-02 | Statistical support = the unadjusted 95% interval above zero, with Holm p-values (from a shifted bootstrap) reported alongside | For the four declared comparisons, statistical support requires the 95% interval above zero and a Holm-adjusted one-sided p-value below 0.025. The p-value now comes from the same bootstrap distribution as the interval (the share of resamples with no reduction). | Changed | The review found that the Holm adjustment was reported but never used, and that the shifted p-value was a different test from the interval. Synthetic runs showed the two could disagree, which would have put "survives costs" next to an interval that includes zero. |
| 20 | 2026-11-02 | Freeze hashes eight source modules | The freeze also hashes `config.py`, `simulation.py`, `synthetic.py`, and the code cells of notebooks 02 and 05 to 09. The outcome mapping moved from notebook 08 into `evaluation.classify_outcome`. A record that no longer matches is replaced automatically only while no test-period computation has been logged. | Changed | The review showed that the outcome rule and the regime and robustness definitions lived in notebook code that the freeze did not cover, so they could have been edited after seeing results without detection. |
| 21 | 2026-11-02 | NMDB-only robustness run that restricts only the training months | The run blanks the refinancing share before NMDB data exist, so neither the training pairs nor the hybrid's average share ḡ use the proxy. Until 24 NMDB training pairs exist, the economic model uses the static duration and the hybrid equals the updating hedge, and these decisions are counted. | Changed | The review found proxy-era days carrying up to 39% of ḡ's weight at the first test decision (long half-lives), and a comparison window that silently shrank. |
| 22 | 2026-11-02 | (implementation details) | NMDB quarters missing a chosen market are dropped instead of reweighted. Costs from partial first and last weeks are carried into the adjacent kept week. A month counts as complete only when its final trading day is in the data (December 31 counts as a trading day even when the federal calendar observes New Year's Day on it). The break-even cost reports which side of it the challenger wins. The look-ahead check treats a value that appears or disappears as a change. The run stops if MBB's rebuilt total return drifts from the provider's adjusted close by more than 10 bp a year, and a VMBS or PMBS series that fails the same check is excluded with its reason recorded. | Fixed | Defects found by the review, each now covered by a unit test. None had affected a real-data result, because none had been computed. |
| 23 | 2026-11-02 | Dimson lag described as absorbing the 3:30 pm yield fix against the 4 pm ETF close | The lag absorbs late ETF pricing. The yield-fix timing creates a lead, which notebook 05 measures on development data. The main specification is unchanged and the effect is listed as limitation L13. | Corrected | The review pointed out that the original rationale had the timing backwards. Every hedge uses the same estimator, so a missing lead shifts the level of all hedges by a similar share. |
| 24 | 2026-11-02 | Updating half-life chosen from {21, 42, 63, 126, 252} trading days, proxy half-life from {2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8} years | Grids extended to {5, 10, 21, 42, 63, 126, 252} days and {1, 1.5, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8} years | Refined | The first development run (real data, 2007–2014 only, no test-period result computed) chose 21 days and 2 years, the shortest values offered, with the updating hedge's objective still falling at 21 days. A choice at the edge of its grid may be under-tuned, and an under-tuned updating hedge would flatter the hybrid. The freeze was replaced automatically because no test evaluation had been logged. |
| 25 | 2026-11-02 | PIMCO represented by a short descriptive PMBS comparison and an optional run on user-supplied PTRIX history | PIMCO's GNMA fund (PDMIX, Institutional class, daily NAV from Yahoo since July 1997) is added as a pre-declared held-out fund. ## Mapping of the design PDF's hypotheses

| PDF item | Status here | Where |
|---|---|---|
| H1 Existence (Cochran's Q, DerSimonian–Laird τ, bootstrap null) | Retained with Q, τ, and simulated calibration of Q. The block-bootstrap null on real data and the Andrews sup-F break test are dropped because they do not change the hedge decision. | Notebooks 04, 05 |
| H2 Direction (duration falls as incentive rises) | Retained as a development-period descriptive relationship. Its forecasting value is tested only through walk-forward models. | Notebook 05 |
| H3 Negative convexity (concave hedged P&L, C = D² − dD/dy) | Retained as (a) the deterministic teaching example and (b) a descriptive quadratic regression of monthly static-hedged returns, interpreted with care because spread moves also co-move with large rate moves. The duration-drift identity check is deferred, because monthly duration estimates are too noisy to identify dD/dy reliably. | Notebooks 03, 08 |
